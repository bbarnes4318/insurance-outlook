Executive summary

The agency runs two lines side by side: Final Expense, sold year-round, and Medicare, sold during the 5-month enrollment season. Over three years it grows to $22.5M in annual revenue and $9.1M in annual net profit, for a cumulative $38.3M revenue and $14.2M net profit. If the partners sold at the end of Year 3, a buyer would pay about $47.6M.

  • Team: Final Expense grows from 5 to 60 agents. Medicare staffs 25, 50 and 100 agents in Years 1, 2 and 3.
  • Profitability: margin climbs from 22% in Year 1 to 41% in Year 3 as held-back commissions, renewals and Medicare residuals stack up.
  • Cash: every month is cash-positive from month 1. The lowest month nets about $24.7K.
  • Partners: with four equal 25% splits, each partner earns about $3.56M pre-tax in profit over the three years.
  • Exit value: the agency is worth about $2.84M at the end of Year 1, $21.36M at the end of Year 2 and $47.65M at the end of Year 3. Selling at the end of Year 3 puts about $63.9M in the partners' hands in total: profit already taken, the sale price, and Final Expense commissions still owed. That's $16.0M per partner.
  • Keep building: each extra year adds more than the year before. Waiting from Year 1 to Year 2 adds $23.5M, and from Year 2 to Year 3 adds $36.2M.
  • What moves the price most: lapse rates. Cutting Final Expense or Medicare lapse by 5 points adds about $4.0M each to the Year 3 price.

How the growth works

  • Final Expense leads grow every quarter. The plan starts at 100 calls a day and adds 100 each quarter, reaching 1,200 by month 34. At 20 calls per agent, that is 5 agents on day one and 60 by the end.
  • Commissions arrive in two parts. 75% of the $780 first-year commission is advanced when the policy places. The remaining 25% is paid in policy months 10–12 on policies still active. That is why Year 1 margin is lower and later years are richer.
  • Renewals and residuals build a book. Final Expense renews at 5% a year on retained policies. Medicare policies that stay on the books (80%) pay a residual the following year, so the Medicare line jumps from a 10% margin in Year 1 to 41% in Year 3.
  • Medicare is seasonal. Agents sell 5 months a year: Oct, Nov, Jan, Feb and Mar.
  • Beyond Year 3. About $2.0M of Final Expense commissions earned in the plan is still owed after month 36. It belongs to the partners whether they keep the agency or sell it.

2What the agency would sell for

At the end of each year the tool prices the agency the way a buyer would. It looks at the agency two ways and takes the higher one; the two are never added together.

As a book of renewals

What the policies already in force will pay in the next 12 months, times a book multiple. Used by FMOs and larger agencies buying a book.

Medicare renewals × 2.0  +  FE renewals × 1.5

On earnings

The year's net profit, minus the overhead a buyer adds (8% of revenue), times a multiple that rises with size. Used by PE-backed aggregators and platforms.

under $1M: 4x · $1–3M: 5.5x · $3–10M: 6.5x · $10M+: 8x

These are planning estimates, not an appraisal. The multiples reflect Final Expense and Medicare agency deals, which trade below property and casualty platforms (around 11.8x at $1M+ EBITDA) because their revenue depends on continued lead spend and on carrier and CMS commission rules.

3About the Insurance Outlook tool

Insurance Outlook is a web app that models the agency's Final Expense and Medicare business over 36 months and recalculates every number instantly when an assumption changes. It has three pages, switched with the toggle at the top of the screen: Planner, ✦ Income goal and ◆ Exit value.

Planner: "What will this plan produce?"

Works forward: set the assumptions and see revenue, costs, profit and partner payouts.

  • Assumptions panel (left). Sliders and number boxes in three tabs: Final Expense, Medicare and Company. The Company tab now includes the Exit valuation settings. A small colored dot marks any value changed from the default; click it to reset that value.
  • Period cards (top). Net profit and revenue for Year 1, Year 2, Year 3 and All 3 years. Each year card shows an Exit tag with the sale price at that year-end, and the All 3 years card names the best year to sell. Click a tag to jump to the Exit value page for that year.
  • What it takes (middle). One row per business line: agents → calls → applications → policies → revenue → costs → net profit. Hover Revenue or Costs for the breakdown.
  • Charts (bottom left). Cash flow (with a Month detail table), Team, Profit by year, Medicare, and Table (the full 3-year profit and loss).
  • Partner payouts (bottom right). Edit each partner's name and split. Splits must add to 100%.

Income goal: "What does it take to earn this?"

Works backward: enter the monthly take-home you want and see the business needed to produce it: agents, calls a day, applications and policies, monthly revenue and costs, and what the agency keeps. It also shows when the current plan reaches that volume, the leaner first nine months, and the extra income renewals and residuals add from Year 2.

For example, a $25,000-a-month goal for Partner 1 (25% split, 80% Final Expense mix) needs the agency to net $100,000 a month: about 10 Final Expense agents on 194 calls a day, plus 38 Medicare agents in season. The current plan reaches that Final Expense volume in month 4.

Exit value: "What would we sell for?" NEW

Prices the agency at the end of Year 1, 2 and 3 and shows what the partners would take home.

  • Year-end picker (top left). Three cards with the sale price at each year-end and whether it is priced on earnings or as a book. Click one to select it.
  • The number. The sale price for the selected year, its low-to-high range, the multiple used and the likely buyer.
  • The deal. Cash at close versus the 24-month earnout, the Final Expense commissions the sellers keep, and each partner's share and cash at close.
  • How a buyer prices you. The earnings and book calculations side by side. The winner is marked "This is your number"; the other shows how much less that offer would be.
  • Sell now or keep building. A chart of the total the partners walk away with at each year-end, and how much one more year adds.
  • Next multiple, Biggest levers, Locked-in renewals. How much more profit reaches the next multiple, the three improvements worth most to the price, and renewals already in force that a buyer should pay for.

4How to use the tool

Start on the Planner to test the plan, use Income goal to turn a personal target into staffing and lead numbers, and use Exit value to see what the work is worth to a buyer.

Planner: test a scenario

  1. Open the tool. It loads on the Planner page with the default plan.
  2. In the Assumptions panel, pick a tab (Final Expense, Medicare or Company).
  3. Drag a slider or type a value and press Enter. Everything updates immediately.
  4. Click a period card to see that period in the chain and partner table.
  5. Read the What it takes chain left to right to see how agents turn into net profit.
  6. Switch chart views to check monthly Cash flow and Team growth. Use Month detail for month-by-month Final Expense numbers.
  7. In Partner payouts, enter names and splits (they must total 100%). Set a Tax / reserve holdback on the Company tab to see after-tax figures.

Income goal: work back from a target

  1. Click ✦ Income goal at the top.
  2. Type the monthly take-home you want, or click a preset.
  3. Under Whose paycheck?, pick the partner. Their split comes from the Planner.
  4. Slide the profit mix between Final Expense and Medicare.
  5. Read The recipe for the agents, calls, applications and policies needed each workday.
  6. If the goal is not reachable, a policy is losing money at current assumptions. Go back to the Planner and lower call costs or payouts, or raise conversion.

Exit value: price the agency

  1. Click ◆ Exit value at the top, or click an Exit tag on a Planner period card.
  2. Pick End of Year 1, 2 or 3.
  3. Read the price and range, and check which lens set it in How a buyer prices you.
  4. In The deal, check cash at close, the earnout and each partner's share.
  5. Use Sell now or keep building to compare the total at each year-end.
  6. To test a different deal, open the Planner's Company tab and change the book multiples, overhead buyers deduct, cash at close or tax on sale.
  7. Use Biggest levers to decide which operating rates to work on first.

5Keeping the plan current

Review the plan each quarter. Replace the default conversion, placement, lapse and cost-per-call rates with the agency's actual results, then share the updated scenario with partners using Copy share link.

  • Track lapse first. Final Expense lapse (30%) and Medicare lapse (20%) have the biggest effect on both yearly profit and the sale price. Each 5-point improvement is worth about $4M at a Year 3 exit.
  • Then cost per call ($7 Final Expense, $10 Medicare) and placement rates.
  • Before any sale conversation, replace the exit assumptions with real terms from brokers or offers (book multiples, overhead, cash at close and tax on sale) so the Exit value page reflects the market, not the defaults.
Exit values are planning estimates based on typical Final Expense and Medicare agency deal multiples, not an appraisal or an offer. Sale figures are pre-tax unless tax on sale is set. Earnouts are paid over 24 months subject to retention.
